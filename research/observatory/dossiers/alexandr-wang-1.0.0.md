## Case definition

This case examines Alexandr Wang’s transition from Scale AI founder and CEO to Meta Chief AI Officer, the institutional design of Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL), Meta’s investment in Scale, Wang’s continuing Scale board relationship, and the limits of inferring ownership or unilateral authority from an executive title.

Proposed headline: **Scale AI founder appointed Meta Chief AI Officer to lead its superintelligence organization after Meta made a non-voting minority investment in Scale.**

## Atomic claims

### AW-C01 — appointment and exact title
Zuckerberg’s memo states that Wang joined Meta as Chief AI Officer and leader of MSL. A later Meta publication retains the title.  
Permissible: **Meta appointed Alexandr Wang Chief AI Officer in 2025 to lead Meta Superintelligence Labs.**  
Restriction: employment start-day precision remains unresolved.

### AW-C02 — company-specific firstness
The reviewed public record did not identify an earlier holder of Meta’s exact Chief AI Officer title.  
Permissible: **Wang is the first publicly documented holder of Meta’s exact Chief AI Officer title in the reviewed record.**  
Prohibited: **Wang was the world’s first Chief AI Officer.**  
Restriction: this is an absence-bounded, company-specific finding, not proof across all internal records or equivalent titles.

### AW-C03 — MSL remit
The appointment memo grouped foundations, product, FAIR and a next-generation-model lab under MSL.  
Permissible: **MSL was announced as an umbrella for Meta’s foundation-model, product, FAIR and next-generation-model work.**  
Restriction: announced scope is not proof of stable operating control.

### AW-C04 — distributed leadership
Nat Friedman was assigned a partnership role spanning AI products and applied research; Shengjia Zhao later became MSL chief scientist.  
Permissible: **Wang’s leadership operated alongside named product, applied-research and scientific leaders.**  
Restriction: do not portray Wang as sole author, scientist or decision-maker for all Meta AI work.

### AW-C05 — Scale investment
Meta’s Q2 filing records a $13.79B non-marketable equity investment in Scale. Scale described the transaction as valuing it above $29B; reporting described about $14.3B for 49%.  
Permissible: **Meta made a roughly $14B investment in Scale, which Scale said valued the company above $29B.**  
Restriction: distinguish SEC carrying amount, reported consideration, reported percentage and company valuation framing.

### AW-C06 — non-control boundary
Meta classifies its Scale interest as non-voting minority and says it lacks significant influence over Scale’s operations.  
Permissible: **Meta reports a non-voting minority Scale stake and says it does not exercise significant influence.**  
Prohibited: **Meta acquired or controls Scale AI.**

### AW-C07 — Scale transition and continuing relationship
Wang left the CEO role, Jason Droege became interim CEO, and Wang remained a Scale director.  
Permissible: **Wang left Scale’s CEO role to join Meta but retained a Scale board seat.**  
Restriction: do not say he severed his Scale relationship or continued to run the company.

### AW-C08 — data and independence representations
Scale said it remained operationally independent and that Meta would not gain access to customer confidential information or internal systems through the transaction.  
Permissible: **Scale represented that the transaction preserved operational independence and existing customer-data protections.**  
Restriction: company representation, not independent audit of implementation.

### AW-C09 — evolving architecture
Reporting documented subsequent changes to Meta’s AI organization, including four groups and Wang-led TBD Lab.  
Permissible: **Meta’s AI organization continued to change after MSL was announced.**  
Restriction: title and remit are time-indexed; do not freeze the launch memo as the permanent structure.

### AW-C10 — ownership/authority synthesis
The case combines founder equity and governance continuity at Scale with appointed executive authority at Meta.  
Epistemic status: classified synthesis.  
Permissible: **Wang’s case distinguishes ownership and board continuity in one institution from delegated executive authority in another.**  
Restriction: interpretive framework only; not a score, causal finding or psychological assessment.

## Contradiction, adverse-evidence and limitations register

- “First Chief AI Officer” is supportable only as first publicly documented holder of Meta’s exact title in the reviewed record, not globally.
- Yann LeCun’s Chief AI Scientist title and Zhao’s MSL Chief Scientist title are different roles and cannot be collapsed into CAIO.
- Meta’s Scale investment is a non-voting minority interest according to Meta’s SEC filing; transaction reporting of 49% does not establish control.
- Scale’s independence and data-access assurances are company representations, not external verification.
- Wang’s current Scale equity, liquidity, voting rights, board committee duties and conflict-management terms are unresolved.
- Announced remit, actual decision rights, budget, hiring authority and later reorganizations require time-specific treatment.
- Appointment and investment are linked in timing, but the record does not support saying Meta “bought” Wang.
- No title establishes product performance, scientific authorship, safety, market success or psychological traits.
