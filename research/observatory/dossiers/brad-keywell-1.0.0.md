## Purpose

This dossier tests the seed hypothesis “entrepreneur, investor, artist and professor.” It separates Brad Keywell’s roles across Uptake, SkillHero, 1+1 Ventures, Lightbank, Groupon, Echo Global Logistics, MediaBank/Mediaocean, WNDR Museum, Chicago Ideas, Future Founders, university teaching and public-company boards. It does not treat those institutions as one company, one cap table, one operating team or one personally owned portfolio.

## Identity resolution

The subject is Bradley A. Keywell, the Michigan-trained lawyer and entrepreneur identified in Groupon and TKO regulatory filings and in institutional biographies. The records consistently connect him to Uptake, Lightbank, Groupon, WNDR Museum and Chicago Ideas. No similarly named person was merged.

## Evidence-backed synopsis

TKO’s 2026 proxy provides the strongest current, date-bounded role record. It identifies Keywell as Uptake’s founder and executive chairman, noting that he served as CEO from 2015 through 2019; SkillHero’s founder, executive chairman and CEO since 2023; founder of 1+1 Ventures; a Lightbank co-founder and former co-managing director from 2012 through 2020; and a TKO director since January 23, 2024. These are distinct corporate and governance relationships. They do not establish complete ownership, cross-entity control, shared contracts, personal economics or sole labor.

Historic filings also require precise ownership language. Groupon’s filings identify Keywell as a co-founder and former director and provide dated beneficial-ownership snapshots that include specified entities, family interests and equity awards. Those records cannot establish his current Groupon position or be carried forward as a present ownership percentage. Groupon’s formation and operation also involved Andrew Mason, Eric Lefkofsky, employees, directors, investors and public shareholders.

Uptake was founded and publicly led by Keywell, but the company had employees, executives, investors, customers and a board. A reported $2.3 billion private financing valuation is a company-level, time-specific transaction metric, not Keywell’s net worth or current company value. Reported layoffs in 2018 and 2019 and the company’s acknowledgment that it had scaled too quickly are material adverse operating evidence. They do not prove personal fault or psychology.

Lightbank’s investments, fund assets and exits belong to investment entities, partners, limited partners, portfolio companies and counterparties. Keywell’s former managing role does not prove personal ownership of portfolio companies or personal receipt of exit values. The same restriction applies to Echo, MediaBank/Mediaocean, DRIVIN and Tempus.

WNDR Museum and Chicago Ideas broaden the portfolio beyond technology. WNDR documents Keywell as founder/curator and attributes individual installations to named artists, including Keywell’s own work. The museum’s sites, employees, artists, licensors and visitors remain separate contributors and rights holders. Chicago Ideas identifies him as founder/chair, while its programming and claimed reach are institutional outputs produced with staff, speakers, partners and audiences.

Institutional profiles describe Keywell as an adjunct professor, author, artist and philanthropist. The available current Booth directory did not independently resolve an active 2026 faculty appointment, so teaching must remain historically and source-bounded rather than treated as a confirmed current post. Artistic self-identification and displayed work establish public artistic activity, not critical quality, ownership of all museum art or a psychological construct.

## Contradictions and adverse evidence

1. First-party biographies collapse many roles into one identity while filings show different entities, dates and governance relationships.
2. Founder, CEO, executive chair, director, investor, fund manager, curator, artist and professor are not synonyms.
3. Groupon’s historic beneficial ownership included security- and entity-specific attribution and cannot be converted into a present holding.
4. Uptake’s private valuation is neither current enterprise value nor personal wealth.
5. Uptake’s workforce reductions and scaling acknowledgment prevent a success-only portfolio narrative.
6. Lightbank assets and portfolio exits belong to funds, partners, investors, companies and counterparties.
7. WNDR’s art, sites and operations include other artists, employees, licensors and institutional labor.
8. Historical teaching evidence does not conclusively establish a current 2026 appointment.
9. No valid psychological measurement was located.
