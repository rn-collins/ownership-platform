## Case definition

This case examines Cathy Hackl’s documented creation and use of the “chief metaverse officer” title, the formation and acquisition of Futures Intelligence Group, her subsequent Journey role, and the shift to Spatial Dynamics/Future Dynamics and broader spatial-computing and AI work. It does not treat publicity language as proof of a global first, equate a title with ownership, or infer commercial outcomes, client causation, technical authorship or psychological traits.

Proposed headline: **Futurist who self-defined a chief metaverse officer role, carried it through an acquisition, and later repositioned her work toward spatial computing and AI.**

## Atomic claims

### CH-C01 — title self-selection
Hackl told Digiday that she chose “chief metaverse officer” while running Futures Intelligence Group.  
Permissible: **Hackl deliberately created or adopted the chief metaverse officer title to describe her work.**  
Restriction: creating a title for oneself is not proof of being the first person worldwide to use it.

### CH-C02 — global-first claim is unverified
Forbes and later profiles described or credited Hackl as the world’s first chief metaverse officer, but the reviewed record supplies no exhaustive prior-art search, registry or dated comparison population.  
Permissible: **Hackl has been widely described as an early or pioneering chief metaverse officer.**  
Prohibited: **She was the world’s first chief metaverse officer.**  
Restriction: “world’s first” remains an attributed publicity claim, not a verified historical conclusion.

### CH-C03 — Futures Intelligence Group
Contemporaneous records identify Hackl as founder/CEO or chief metaverse officer of Futures Intelligence Group, a consulting firm.  
Permissible: **Hackl founded and led Futures Intelligence Group.**  
Restriction: exact legal entity, cap table, founding date inconsistencies, revenue and client outcomes remain unresolved.

### CH-C04 — acquisition
Journey announced acquiring Futures Intelligence Group in May 2022; transaction terms were not disclosed in the reviewed record.  
Permissible: **Journey acquired Futures Intelligence Group in 2022.**  
Restriction: do not publish the reported “seven-figure” value without transaction documentation; price, consideration, retained equity and earnout are unverified.

### CH-C05 — Journey role
Journey identified Hackl as chief metaverse officer and a co-founder in its launch announcement.  
Permissible: **Following the acquisition, Hackl served as Journey’s chief metaverse officer and was identified as a co-founder.**  
Restriction: title does not establish equity percentage, board power, ownership of client work or sole authorship.

### CH-C06 — Spatial Dynamics
A February 2024 issuer announcement described Spatial Dynamics as a newly formed spatial-computing and AI solutions company and Hackl as co-CEO; WEF later described her as CEO.  
Permissible: **Hackl launched and led Spatial Dynamics, repositioning her company work toward spatial computing and AI.**  
Restriction: reconcile co-CEO versus CEO dates; legal entity, ownership, team, proprietary technology and client outcomes remain unresolved.

### CH-C07 — Future Dynamics
Company-controlled profiles describe Future Dynamics as Hackl’s foresight/exploration lab and public-facing vehicle for speaking, thought leadership, books, courses and coaching.  
Permissible: **Hackl presents Future Dynamics as her foresight and thought-leadership lab.**  
Restriction: relationship between Future Dynamics and Spatial Dynamics, legal entities, ownership and operating economics are unresolved.

### CH-C08 — field transition
Independent reporting documents a broader market retreat from “metaverse” executive language and notes Hackl’s 2024 company language emphasized spatial computing and AI.  
Permissible: **Hackl’s public positioning evolved alongside a broader shift from metaverse-specific language toward spatial computing and AI.**  
Restriction: this is temporal description, not proof that she abandoned the metaverse or that market conditions caused her company decisions.

### CH-C09 — role-invention synthesis
The strongest evidence supports purposeful role construction and portability through an acquisition, not historical global firstness.  
Epistemic status: classified synthesis.  
Permissible: **Hackl illustrates how a professional can name an emerging remit, institutionalize it through a company, and carry it into an acquiring organization.**  
Restriction: not a score, causal proof or psychological inference.
