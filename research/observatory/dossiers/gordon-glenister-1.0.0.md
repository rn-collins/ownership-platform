## Purpose

This dossier tests the seed hypothesis that Gordon Glenister “founded BCMA’s influence division.” It separates originating idea, joint institutional decision, launch labor, delegated leadership, legal ownership, later operations, separate ventures, attributed outcomes and psychology.

## Identity resolution

The subject is Gordon Michael Glenister, the UK marketing and membership executive documented as former Director General of the British Promotional Merchandise Association and later Global Head of Influencer Marketing at the Branded Content Marketing Association. Sources referring only to “Gordon Glenister” were retained when organization, title and chronology matched. No similarly named person was merged.

## Evidence-backed synopsis

In a 2024 interview, Glenister said that after leaving BPMA he met BCMA co-founder Andrew Canter, raised the absence of a trade body for influencer marketing, and that “we decided to launch Influence” as a BCMA division. BCMA’s own materials identify the resulting body as the BCMA Influence global chapter and Glenister as Global Head of Influencer Marketing. That supports a meaningful originating and operating role. It does not establish that Glenister independently founded, legally owned or unilaterally controlled the division.

The chapter operated through BCMA’s institutional structure, members, steering group, research, guidelines, accreditation activity, competitions and events. Glenister also developed a podcast, books, consultancy, academy and other products through separately described arrangements. Those properties must not be treated as BCMA assets or vice versa without rights evidence.

## Contradictions and adverse-evidence register

1. “Founded BCMA influence division” conflicts with Glenister’s own plural formation wording: he and Andrew Canter decided to launch it.
2. “His division” would conflict with BCMA’s institutional placement, brand, members, steering group and program infrastructure.
3. A current BCMA title does not establish unchanged authority since 2019; employment contract, reporting line, time allocation and decision rights are unavailable.
4. Self-described founder labels for separate Glenister ventures do not establish formal equity, current control or operating results.
5. Repeated launch claims often descend from Glenister’s own biography or interviews and are one provenance cluster, not multiple independent confirmations.
6. No material litigation or adjudicated misconduct tied to the formation claim was located in the reviewed searches; this negative search is not proof of absence.
