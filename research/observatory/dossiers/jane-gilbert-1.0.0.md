## Case definition

This case examines Jane Gilbert’s 2021 appointment as Miami-Dade County’s first Chief Heat Officer, the institutional design of the role, the county’s heat plan and operational response, the 2025 elimination/absorption of the county position, and Gilbert’s subsequent Chief Heat Ambassador role. It distinguishes a well-corroborated program-origin claim from an impossible-to-prove universal negative.

Proposed headline: **Miami-Dade’s inaugural Chief Heat Officer built a cross-agency heat-governance model later replicated internationally.**

## Atomic claims

### JG-C01 — county appointment
Miami-Dade announced Gilbert as its first-ever Interim Chief Heat Officer in April 2021, with the position initially hosted by The Miami Foundation and connected to Resilient305.  
Permissible: **Miami-Dade appointed Jane Gilbert as its inaugural interim Chief Heat Officer in 2021.**  
Restriction: preserve “interim,” host and county scope when describing the initial appointment.

### JG-C02 — historical firstness
Government, program, WEF and peer-reviewed sources repeatedly call Gilbert the first person in the world to hold the exact Chief Heat Officer title. The appointment also preceded the associated Athens and Freetown appointments within the same initiative.  
Permissible: **The program and multiple institutional sources identify Gilbert’s 2021 appointment as the first Chief Heat Officer appointment worldwide.**  
Restriction: phrase as a highly corroborated attributed historical claim; no global employment-title registry proves that no obscure prior use existed. Do not generalize to “first heat official” or “first official responsible for heat.”

### JG-C03 — remit
The appointment tasked Gilbert with cross-departmental coordination and acceleration of action on health and economic risks from extreme heat.  
Permissible: **The role coordinated heat-risk strategy across county departments, municipalities and external partners.**  
Restriction: do not attribute all county heat activity or outcomes solely to Gilbert.

### JG-C04 — action plan
Miami-Dade published a 2022 Extreme Heat Action Plan organized around three goals and nineteen actions, developed through task-force, expert, stakeholder and resident participation.  
Permissible: **Gilbert co-led a participatory process that produced Miami-Dade’s first Extreme Heat Action Plan.**  
Restriction: plan publication is not proof that every action was implemented or effective.

### JG-C05 — evidence method
A peer-reviewed BAMS article documents local vulnerability and time-series analysis, community engagement and translation into planning.  
Permissible: **The program combined local quantitative analysis and community engagement to inform heat planning.**  
Restriction: this supports method and preliminary implementation, not a causal estimate of the office’s effect.

### JG-C06 — current county program claims
The county’s current heat portal reports a May–October Heat Season protocol and states Miami-Dade has recently had Florida’s lowest rates of heat-related emergency-room visits.  
Permissible: **Miami-Dade now operates a seasonal heat protocol and reports comparatively low recent heat-related ER-visit rates.**  
Restriction: do not claim the Chief Heat Officer caused those rates without a defined period, denominator, comparison method and causal design.

### JG-C07 — office transition
2025 reporting says the standalone Chief Heat Officer position was eliminated, duties were absorbed by the resilience office, and Gilbert planned to retire from county service.  
Permissible: **Miami-Dade folded Chief Heat Officer duties into a reorganized resilience function in 2025.**  
Restriction: role elimination does not itself establish program failure, abandonment or political cause.

### JG-C08 — ambassador role
The Climate Resilience Center appointed Gilbert Chief Heat Ambassador in November 2025 to advise communities and support a heat-resilience exchange.  
Permissible: **After county service, Gilbert became the Climate Resilience Center’s Chief Heat Ambassador.**  
Restriction: organizational title does not imply public authority over participating cities.

### JG-C09 — institutional replication synthesis
Sources document additional Chief Heat Officer appointments after Miami-Dade, supporting diffusion of a named governance model.  
Epistemic status: classified synthesis.  
Permissible: **The named Chief Heat Officer model spread to other jurisdictions after the Miami-Dade appointment.**  
Restriction: diffusion does not prove direct causation, effectiveness or identical authority across jurisdictions.

## Contradiction and limitations register

- The original county release says “county’s first-ever”; later county/program sources escalate to “first person in history/world.” These are compatible but not identical scopes.
- The world-first claim is unusually well corroborated across the originating program and peer-reviewed literature, but remains a universal historical claim without a comprehensive global registry.
- “Interim” is often omitted in retrospectives.
- Current county outcome statements lack enough visible methodological detail for causal attribution.
- City of Miami and Miami-Dade County are distinct jurisdictions; Gilbert’s earlier Chief Resilience Officer role was with the city, while the Chief Heat Officer role was county-level.
- The 2025 reorganization ended the standalone post but retained duties, so “abolished heat work” would be false.
- No psychological attributes are measured.
