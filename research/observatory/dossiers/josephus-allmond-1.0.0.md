## Identity resolution

Josephus M. Allmond is a Virginia attorney and public official serving as Chief Energy Officer in the Office of Governor Abigail D. Spanberger. Before his March 2026 appointment, he worked at the Southern Environmental Law Center on utility-regulatory, solar, energy-policy and environmental-justice matters and served as a citizen member of Virginia's Commission on Electric Utility Regulation. The appointment record, government biography, SELC record, SCC filings and education record consistently identify the same person.

## Seed hypothesis tested

The map calls Allmond “Virginia's first Chief Energy Officer.” Executive Order 14 created the position in the Office of the Governor on March 25, 2026, and the governor appointed Allmond as its inaugural holder. This supports Virginia-specific firstness—not national or worldwide firstness. The order created the office; the public record does not show that Allmond designed the title or personally constructed the role. His authority is cabinet-level but derivative and collaborative: the order reserves the governor's ultimate authority and directs coordination with state secretariats and agencies, the SCC, PJM, utilities, the General Assembly and private stakeholders.

## Contradictions, adverse evidence, and open questions

1. “First Chief Energy Officer” is safe only when expressly limited to Virginia.
2. The governor created the position; no reviewed source establishes that Allmond designed it.
3. “Lead the charge” is appointment rhetoric, not proof of sole authority or labor.
4. The office coordinates bodies it does not own or control, including the SCC, PJM, utilities and legislative institutions.
5. Regulatory filings state administration positions; they are not final orders or proof of resulting savings.
6. Earlier SELC advocacy belongs to clients, teams, coalitions and the institution as well as named lawyers.
7. CEUR membership and nonprofit board service do not imply ownership of institutional assets or output.
8. Current compensation, appointment terms, budget, delegated signature authority and complete personnel structure remain unresolved.
