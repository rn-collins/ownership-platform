## Case definition

This case examines Linda J. Fisher’s 2004–2016 role at DuPont, the historical scope of the Chief Sustainability Officer title, the responsibilities attached to it, DuPont’s market-facing sustainability program, and the limits of inferring company performance from an executive title. It also records Fisher’s movement among environmental regulation, regulated industry, legal practice and corporate sustainability.

Proposed headline: **DuPont executive whose 2004 appointment helped establish the Chief Sustainability Officer title in publicly traded companies.**

## Atomic claims

### LF-C01 — appointment
DuPont appointed Fisher vice president and Chief Sustainability Officer effective July 12, 2004.  
Permissible: **DuPont appointed Linda Fisher vice president and Chief Sustainability Officer in 2004.**  
Restriction: preserve the paired vice-president title and do not infer C-suite reporting line without an organization chart.

### LF-C02 — historical firstness
DuPont later stated that Fisher’s appointment made it the first publicly traded company to name a CSO. Independent and research sources repeat that narrower formulation.  
Permissible: **DuPont identifies Fisher’s 2004 appointment as the first Chief Sustainability Officer appointment by a publicly traded company.**  
Prohibited: **Fisher was the world’s first Chief Sustainability Officer.**  
Restriction: the evidence does not exclude earlier use by private companies, nonprofits, universities, governments or differently titled equivalent executives.

### LF-C03 — remit
Sources assign Fisher responsibility for sustainable growth, environment and health, product stewardship, global regulation and, in later formulations, safety and public policy.  
Permissible: **Fisher’s DuPont remit combined sustainability strategy with environmental, health, product-stewardship and regulatory responsibilities.**  
Restriction: remit changed over time and does not prove final decision authority across all business units.

### LF-C04 — market-facing goals
DuPont says Fisher’s tenure included its first market-facing sustainability goals in 2006, linking R&D and revenue growth to defined environmental benefits.  
Permissible: **Under Fisher’s leadership, DuPont introduced market-facing sustainability goals tied to product innovation and revenue.**  
Restriction: leadership contribution is not sole causation; definitions, baselines and assurance must accompany numerical claims.

### LF-C05 — governance process
Fisher described annual sustainability reviews with business leadership and an eleven-criterion product-development screen.  
Permissible: **Fisher described mechanisms for integrating sustainability into portfolio review and new-product evaluation.**  
Restriction: interview description does not establish universal compliance, audit results or decision outcomes.

### LF-C06 — company-reported results
DuPont reported emissions, water, R&D and product-revenue progress against its goals.  
Permissible: **DuPont reported progress against several footprint and market-facing goals during Fisher’s tenure.**  
Restriction: retain “company-reported”; do not combine unlike revenue categories, assume additionality, or treat targets as independently audited impact.

### LF-C07 — PFOA adverse evidence
PFOA controversy and regulatory scrutiny were active when Fisher joined DuPont and remained relevant during her sustainability tenure. Fisher later described dialogue and a phaseout proposal.  
Permissible: **Fisher’s tenure included management of sustainability claims amid material PFOA controversy and phaseout efforts.**  
Restriction: do not attribute pre-appointment conduct personally to Fisher; do not equate dialogue or phaseout with resolution of liability, remediation or health effects.

### LF-C08 — retirement and succession
Fisher retired from DuPont in February 2016 and Krysta Harden succeeded her.  
Permissible: **Fisher retired in 2016 after roughly eleven and a half years, and DuPont appointed Krysta Harden as successor.**  
Restriction: exact final employment date should follow personnel records if day precision is needed.

### LF-C09 — career boundary
Fisher’s documented career spans senior EPA roles, Monsanto government affairs, Latham & Watkins, DuPont and later boards.  
Permissible: **Fisher moved between environmental regulation, regulated industry, legal practice, corporate sustainability and governance roles.**  
Restriction: chronology does not establish conflicts, capture, impropriety or a stable personal ideology.

### LF-C10 — institutionalization synthesis
The appointment is evidence of a company formalizing sustainability as an executive remit; it is not evidence that the company achieved sustainability.  
Epistemic status: classified synthesis.  
Permissible: **Fisher’s case separates the institutionalization of sustainability authority from the empirical evaluation of corporate conduct.**  
Restriction: interpretive framework only; not a score or causal finding.

## Contradiction, adverse-evidence and limitations register

- The seed’s “world’s first” wording overstates the evidence. The supportable population is publicly traded companies.
- Exact-title firstness differs from first executive performing equivalent sustainability work.
- Fisher was a vice president with a CSO title; “C-suite” status and CEO access must not be assumed.
- DuPont’s reported goal performance is company-authored and requires definition-level verification.
- PFOA controversy prevents using the appointment as a proxy for company sustainability.
- The CSO remit combined sustainability and compliance-related functions, complicating comparisons with later standalone CSOs.
- Career transitions across regulator, corporation and law firm are observable; motive and psychology are not.
- Current board affiliations need entity-by-entity and date-specific confirmation.
