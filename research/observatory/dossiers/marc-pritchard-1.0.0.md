## Identity resolution

Marc S. Pritchard is the current Chief Brand Officer of The Procter & Gamble Company. P&G identifies him as responsible for its worldwide brand-building disciplines and multibillion-dollar media, marketing and advertising strategies. Public records distinguish that current corporate office from his earlier P&G finance, brand, business-unit, strategy and chief-marketing roles; his former chairmanship and current directorship at the Association of National Advertisers; and his separate Vital Voices board service. The record does not establish personal ownership of P&G, its brands, trademarks, campaigns, data, budgets, results or industry systems.

## Seed hypothesis tested

The map identifies Pritchard as a role-building chief brand executive. The record supports a long internal career, a 2008 appointment as P&G's chief marketing officer, a later Global Brand Building Officer title and the Chief Brand Officer title by 2014. It also supports material public leadership on media transparency, brand safety, multicultural marketing, creativity and AI-assisted brand building. It does not establish that Pritchard created or negotiated the Chief Brand Officer role, invented P&G's brand-building system, or personally owns its outputs. P&G's board and executive structure, business units, brand teams, employees, agencies, platforms, partners and shareholders remain separately attributed. `roleBuiltFlag=false`.

## Contradictions, adverse evidence and open questions

1. “Chief marketing officer,” “Global Brand Building Officer” and “Chief Brand Officer” are time-bounded P&G titles, not interchangeable current offices.
2. The record supports role progression but does not show that Pritchard created, negotiated or uniquely originated the Chief Brand Officer role.
3. Current ANA records identify Pritchard as a director, not chair.
4. Responsibility for multibillion-dollar strategy does not make the corporate budget his personal spending or property.
5. P&G or its subsidiaries—not Pritchard personally—hold the referenced brand trademarks.
6. Brand work depends on business-unit leaders, employees, agencies, creators, platforms, retailers and partners whose labor and rights must remain visible.
7. Digital-spend reductions and media-transparency advocacy do not establish Pritchard's sole causation of savings or industry reform.
8. The 2020 media review and decision not to disclose individual advertising choices are bounded corporate-policy evidence, not proof of personal misconduct or platform control.
9. The 2024 Olympics shift demonstrates strategy change, not failure of earlier campaigns or proof of Pritchard-specific performance.
10. “Force for growth and force for good,” multicultural-marketing, creativity and AI claims remain attributed propositions unless separately validated.
11. Company and industry awards cannot be converted into sole personal achievement.
12. Compensation, employment terms, beneficial ownership, campaign and framework IP, agency contracts, media rights, complete labor allocation, audited individual impact, causality and psychology remain unresolved.
