## 1. Defensible replacement

> Pieter Levels publicly documents a large lifetime portfolio of software projects, most of which failed, ended, or were noncommercial. He describes his principal businesses—including Nomads.com/Nomad List, Remote OK, Photo AI and Interior AI—as self-funded and primarily built and operated by him, while also disclosing limited contractor support. No complete public entity map, cap table, or financing record was located.

## 2. What the evidence actually measures

The case is useful for studying a particular operating model:

- one principal maker across multiple products;
- repeated low-cost experimentation;
- automation substituted for employee headcount;
- limited specialist contractors;
- revenue-funded product development;
- a portfolio containing successful businesses, failed launches, experiments and noncommercial work.

It does **not** establish that Levels personally owns every relevant legal entity, every product has separate economic value, nobody else contributed, or every revenue figure is audited.

## 5. Product-count taxonomy

A defensible Observatory count must use mutually exclusive states:

| State | Definition | Inclusion in current operating portfolio |
|---|---|---|
| Active material business | Live, monetized and materially maintained | Yes |
| Active small product | Live and monetized but not demonstrated material | Separately |
| Active noncommercial project | Live without a profit objective | No |
| New experiment | Too early to classify | No |
| Dormant/discontinued | No longer materially operated | No |
| Failed launch | Did not achieve stated traction/revenue | No |
| Merged predecessor | Absorbed into another product | Historical only |
| Exited/transferred | Ownership transferred | No; record event separately |

His first-party ledger is evidence for his own classification, not a substitute for an independent status audit.

## 6. Operating-labor taxonomy

“Solo” must be decomposed:

- principal founder/maker;
- full-time employees;
- part-time employees;
- independent contractors;
- moderators/community labor;
- on-call/SLA specialists;
- vendors and cloud/model providers;
- unpaid user contributions and community-generated data;
- AI services and automated agents.

The reviewed record supports **one principal full-time operator with disclosed contractor and vendor support**, not “no team” in the literal sense.

## 7. Funding taxonomy

“No VC” must be kept separate from:

- no institutional equity;
- no angel equity;
- no debt;
- no grants;
- no accelerator capital;
- no revenue-based financing;
- no friends-and-family capital;
- customer prepayments;
- personal savings;
- retained earnings;
- cloud credits or in-kind support.

The reviewed evidence directly supports Levels’s repeated self-description of bootstrapping. It does not independently prove every zero-capital category for every product and legal entity.

## 8. Contradiction and uncertainty register

1. **40+ versus 100+ lifetime entries.** The current ledger exceeds 100 entries, but most are not current commercial products.
2. **Solo versus contractors.** Levels has disclosed three human support functions. “No employees” and “no human assistance” are not equivalent.
3. **Built versus owned.** First-person creation accounts do not prove present legal title, IP assignment or cap-table ownership.
4. **No VC versus no outside capital.** His public claim concerns venture capital/bootstrapping; other financing categories require separate evidence.
5. **Revenue versus profit.** Public figures are self-reported and sometimes annualized; gross revenue, net revenue, MRR, ARR and profit must remain distinct.
6. **Product versus business.** A site, side project, music release, event, investment return and commercial SaaS cannot share one count.
7. **Automation versus independence.** Automated operation still depends on payment processors, hosting, APIs, model providers and contractors.
8. **levels.vc naming.** It is his label for angel investing, and his site expressly says it is not an actual investment fund.
