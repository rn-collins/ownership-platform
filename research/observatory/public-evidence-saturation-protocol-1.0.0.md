# Public-evidence saturation protocol

Version: 1.0.0
Effective: 2026-07-25
Applies to: Institutions of One Observatory case research

## Objective

The Observatory aims for public-evidence saturation: a reproducible search across the material public record until additional documented searches produce no new material claims, relationships, events, contradictions, or source classes.

Saturation is not a claim that every page on the internet has been observed. Unindexed, deleted, private, jurisdiction-blocked, dynamically inaccessible, or undisclosed records may exist. The permissible completion statement is: **No additional material public evidence was discovered after the documented saturation searches as of the stated cutoff.**

## Required search dimensions

Every flagship dossier must search:

1. Exact name, full-name variants, initials, misspellings, former names when publicly documented, and name plus each organization.
2. Employer and institutional domains, newsrooms, blogs, leadership pages, product pages, press releases, archived pages, investor relations, regulatory filings, and event records.
3. Independent journalism, specialist trade publications, transcripts, podcasts, video, conference programs, books, academic or professional profiles, awards, nonprofit records, and public social posts.
4. Each documented title, product, organization, program, collaborator, launch, transition, and quoted distinctive phrase.
5. Date-bounded searches for each known career period.
6. Disconfirming searches: departure, correction, dispute, lawsuit, criticism, failure, closure, demotion, replacement, title conflict, and contradictory chronology.
7. Primary-source chasing from derivative coverage until the earliest accessible underlying record is located or recorded as unavailable.
8. Public legal and regulatory sources appropriate to the case and jurisdiction, without implying that absence from a database proves absence of an event.

## Source handling

Each source receives:
- stable source ID;
- title, publisher, author when available, URL, publication date, access date and source class;
- primary/independent/derivative status;
- person/company attribution;
- accessibility state;
- archive URL or content fingerprint when legally and technically available;
- the exact atomic claims it supports, contradicts or merely repeats.

Multiple articles repeating one press release constitute one provenance cluster, not independent corroboration. Search snippets are discovery aids, not final evidence when the underlying source can be reviewed.

## Claim standard

Claims must be atomic, dated when possible and limited to what the source establishes. Each claim records:
- subject, predicate and object;
- temporal scope;
- source links and provenance clusters;
- epistemic status;
- confidence rationale;
- permissible wording;
- prohibited inference;
- contradiction status;
- materiality and construct mapping.

Titles do not prove decision rights. Company outcomes do not prove personal causation. Employment does not prove ownership. First-person statements establish that the person made the statement, not necessarily the external truth of every assertion. Psychological attributes remain unmeasured unless collected through an approved research instrument.

## Search ledger

The dossier must include the exact query families, databases/domains, date searched, result disposition, inaccessible results and follow-up searches. Negative searches are recorded but never converted into proof of absence.

## Saturation tests

A case can be marked saturated only when:
- every known entity, title, product, relationship and event has been recursively searched;
- every material claim has primary evidence, genuinely independent corroboration, or an explicit limitation;
- derivative sources have been clustered;
- contradictions and adverse evidence have been searched;
- inaccessible/paywalled/deleted leads are registered;
- at least two final search passes using new query combinations yield no new material evidence;
- a researcher records a dated saturation decision.

## Coverage reporting

Report separate percentages for source-class coverage, chronology coverage, claim support, relationship support, event support, contradiction search and construct observability. Never collapse these into a psychometric score or a claim that the internet is complete.

## Versioning

New evidence creates a new dossier and review-package version. Earlier versions remain immutable. No evidence expansion silently changes a published claim. Publication requires an accountable review decision, and classified syntheses remain distinct from observed claims.
